2025 – 2026Tongji UniversityResearch project

5G Packet Reception on a High-Speed Train

A MATLAB tool that measures the latency of every 5G packet, built on top of a method that emulates high-speed trains without boarding one.

Read more

Carried out during my exchange in Shanghai, as part of my specialisation in information theory and mobile communication systems. Measuring on a real high-speed train is expensive, hard to repeat and easily disturbed (weather, a vehicle passing nearby…). My supervisor, José Rodríguez-Piñeiro, developed a method that emulates high speeds by interpolating the OFDM signal in time before sending it: the subcarriers get closer together, so the receiver suffers the same Doppler effects as on a fast train, while measuring at low speed.

My part was to measure packet latency in this setting, which standard MATLAB 5G examples do not provide (they focus on throughput). I studied the HARQ protocol (ACK/NACK, retransmissions, redundancy versions) and wrote a MATLAB class that plugs into MathWorks' 5G NR downlink example with only a few added lines. It follows each packet from its first transmission to its successful decoding, and records its delay, its retransmission history and the packets lost after every retry, here with a 200 Hz Doppler shift to match train conditions.

With 30 kHz subcarrier spacing (0.5 ms slots) and 16 HARQ processes, each retransmission adds about 8 ms of latency. The class is generic: it can be reused in any slot-based simulation (4G, Wi-Fi, queues…), and I wrote its documentation for future projects.

  • 5G NR
  • HARQ
  • Latency

2025 – 2026Tongji UniversityIndividual research project

Simulating a Digital Transmission Channel

A Python simulation of a complete digital communication chain: handwritten digits (MNIST) are compressed, encoded, modulated and sent through a noisy channel, then a neural network checks whether they can still be recognised.

Read more

Carried out for the Information Theory and Applications course. Each image becomes a bitstream, compressed with Huffman coding (source coding), protected with a repetition code (channel coding), modulated in 4-QAM and sent over a noisy channel (AWGN or binary symmetric) before being decoded and rebuilt.

Huffman brings the bit rate close to the source's entropy, but a single flipped bit can wreck a whole image (catastrophic error propagation). A CNN (99.2 % accuracy on clean images) measured the impact: at 5 dB SNR, it recognises 92 % of digits sent with 5 repetitions against 16 % without protection. Fixed-length coding turned out more robust: always a trade-off between compression and reliability.

Along the way I learned to handle large datasets and to put into practice information theory, data compression, source and channel coding, modulation and the effect of noise on a transmission.

Network Energy Analysis of a Smartphone

How much energy does a phone really spend on TikTok, streaming or a call, and how much of it goes to the cellular radio? We answered it by measuring real phones in everyday use, then turned the results into a web simulator.

Read more

We recorded how much power the phones drew during real use, keeping the radio part apart from the rest of the device. The scenarios covered short videos, streaming and voice/video calls on 3G, LTE and 5G, both standing still and on a moving tram in Lyon.

My part: processing multi-million-row recordings in Python (processed in chunks, interactive Plotly charts) and the comparative analysis of app × network × mobility. Scrolling short videos costs about 0.04–0.06 Wh per minute (~3 Wh per hour); no network was consistently cheaper and mobility had no clear effect, with only 1–3 sessions per setup, so these are trends rather than proof.

The results feed a web simulator (Node.js/Express) that estimates the energy, battery percentage and CO₂ of a day of phone use. I added the device battery specs and front-end fixes.

2025INSA LyonTeam project

Facial Expression Recognition with AI

A small neural network that reads 7 facial expressions (happy, sad, surprise…) live from a webcam, and a hands-on lesson in how much an AI model depends on its data.

Read more

OpenCV finds the face in the image, crops it to 48×48 grayscale, and a convolutional neural network built with TensorFlow / Keras gives a probability for each expression. It reaches 56.8 % accuracy on the FER2013 test set (humans score around 65 %): very good on happy, neutral and surprise, much weaker on disgust and fear.

A large part of the work was on the data: the disgust class had ~400 training images against ~7,000 for happy, so we enriched it with augmentation (contrast and brightness variations). We also photographed ourselves making each expression to test and enrich the model.

Overall, I learned to build, train and evaluate models with TensorFlow, to evaluate them with key AI concepts like accuracy and the confusion matrix, and above all that a model is only as good as its data: faces that look different from the training set, or rare classes, stay poorly recognised however the model is tuned.

  • TensorFlow / Keras

Call for Tenders: Redesigning a Département's Network

As a team of six, we answered a realistic public call for tenders: redesigning the whole network of the Bas-Rhin département, from reading the specifications to the priced proposal and its final presentation.

Read more

Based on a real public tender, the Bas-Rhin département wanted to modernise the network linking all its sites: replace the old ATM loop of its metropolitan network, rebuild the LAN of its IT department and main and annex sites, connect its 117 Maisons du Rhin, and add secure Wi-Fi and IP telephony. Acting as an operator with HPE Aruba as equipment supplier, we proposed an SD-WAN architecture with triple redundancy (dedicated link, MPLS tunnel over 4G, ERPS loop as backup), rolled out in four phases over about 15 months.

As business engineer, I was in charge of pricing: choosing the technical references (switches, routers, firewalls, Wi-Fi access points, IP-PBX), counting the equipment needed on each site, coordinating with the supplier and building the cost breakdown, for a total of about €1 million excluding VAT (equipment, labour, spares, training and maintenance).

Overall, I learned to read a long technical specification and turn it into client needs, to write technical documents (technical proposal, architecture and project deliverables), to work as a team with clear roles and deadlines, and to present and defend an offer in front of the client's representatives.
